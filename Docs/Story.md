# Story

World lore and narrative design for Airk.

This is not a linear story. Airk is a sandbox — the player explores freely, and narratives emerge from the world.

---

## Setting: Kreznik

Kreznik is a sprawling cyberpunk metropolis. Millions of people layered vertically: corporate spires pierce the smog above, neon-lit streets pulse at ground level, and a forgotten undercity festers below.

Neural interfaces are ubiquitous. Everyone is connected, and everyone is vulnerable. Hacking, neural manipulation, and data theft are facts of life. Corporations sell security. The streets sell survival.

Multiple megacorps compete for control. No single entity owns Kreznik — it's a contested territory of overlapping power structures, uneasy truces, and constant low-level conflict.

---

## Player Start

Blank slate. You wake up in an alley with nothing — no money, no gear, no backstory.

The game doesn't tell you who you are. Maybe you were robbed. Maybe you had a rough night. Maybe something worse happened. It doesn't matter. What matters is what you do next.

The world is there to explore. Stories find you, not the other way around.

---

## Factions

### NeoCortex Corp
Neural technology corporation. Largest provider of neural interfaces and implant maintenance in Kreznik. Publicly respectable, privately ruthless. Runs the secret Project Icarus — a mass neural suppression program targeting the district. One power player among several corps, but the one with the deepest reach into people's heads.

### The Circuit
Underground network of fixers, hackers, smugglers, and information brokers. Chrome's world. No central leadership — it's a web of trust, favors, and mutual profit. Morally gray. They'll help you for the right price, and sell you out for a better one.

### Free Clinics
Independent medics and neurotechs operating outside corporate control. Kira's world. They patch up street-level people who can't afford (or can't trust) corporate healthcare. Underfunded, overworked, quietly defiant. Some have their own secrets.

### Street Gangs
Territorial groups fighting over resources, turf, and reputation. Not a single faction — multiple gangs with different agendas. Potential allies, enemies, or employers depending on your choices and their current mood.

### CorpSec
Corporate private military. Not tied to one corp — CorpSec contracts with whoever pays. They guard corporate zones, enforce corporate interests, and don't ask questions. High-level enemies with good equipment.

## Main Arc: Project Icarus

An optional, discoverable conspiracy.

**What it is**: NeoCortex is running a mass neural suppression program. They can remotely dampen, alter, or erase neural activity across entire districts through the neural interface network everyone depends on.

**How the player finds it**: not through a backstory reveal. Through the world — rumors from NPCs, encrypted data on stolen devices, patterns that don't add up, people who suddenly aren't themselves. It's there if you look for it. It's invisible if you don't.

**Arc structure**:
1. Hear rumors — scattered, contradictory, easy to dismiss
2. Investigate — find evidence, talk to people who know things, break into places
3. Infiltrate — the Icarus facility exists in the Outer Rim. Getting in is a challenge.
4. Choose outcome — expose it, destroy it, leverage it, walk away. Player decides.

**Completely optional**. A player who never investigates Icarus can have a full, satisfying experience just surviving and thriving in Kreznik.

## World Zones

Target: 100+ rooms total, 30-80 per zone. Built out gradually.

### Sector 7 (exists — expand)
Starting area. Street-level Kreznik. Neon-lit alleys, dive bars, street markets, cheap noodle stands. Low difficulty. This is where new players learn the ropes, find their first jobs, and get their first coffin hotel room.

### Outer Rim (exists — expand)
Industrial outskirts. Warehouses, abandoned factories, the Icarus facility behind blast doors. Medium difficulty. The edge of civilized Kreznik — fewer people, more danger, more secrets.

### Undercity (planned)
Below street level. Abandoned metro tunnels, collapsed infrastructure, gang hideouts, black market dens. Medium-high difficulty, good loot. Dark, dangerous, and full of people who don't want to be found.

### Corporate District (planned)
The towers above. Executive offices, high-security labs, luxury residences. High difficulty, best loot. Getting in requires clearance, connections, or audacity. Metro Blue Line access.

### Docklands (planned)
Trade hub on Kreznik's edge. Warehouses, smuggling routes, shipping containers, seedy bars. Medium difficulty. Economy-focused — best place to buy, sell, and trade. Smuggler contacts.

### The Nexus (planned)
Central hub. Metro interchange connecting all lines. Major shops, services, information brokers. Safe zone — no combat. The crossroads of Kreznik where all paths meet.

## Sandbox Philosophy

- **Story exists in the world, not on rails.** NPCs have their own problems and agendas. The player encounters narrative by being present, not by following a quest marker.
- **Quests are opportunities, not obligations.** Someone asks for help. You can help, refuse, or exploit the situation. No quest log pushing you forward.
- **Multiple solutions.** Fight, hack, talk, sneak, bribe. Most problems have more than one answer. The "right" answer depends on your build and your ethics.
- **The world is indifferent.** Kreznik doesn't care about you. It was here before you woke up in that alley, and it'll be here after you're gone. That's what makes your choices matter — nothing is scripted for your benefit.
- **Discovery over exposition.** The game doesn't explain itself. You learn by exploring, talking, failing, and paying attention.
